The basics

What contrast actually is.

1
A helper, not a dyeIt makes certain tissues and blood vessels show up more clearly. It doesn't stain your organs.
2
Given by IV or as a drinkCT uses an iodine-based contrast, MRI uses gadolinium. Some CT scans use a drink instead.
3
Your body clears itIt passes out naturally, mostly in urine. Extra water afterward helps.

Source: RadiologyInfo.org, from the ACR and RSNA.

Myth vs fact

3 contrast myths, busted.

1
Myth: shellfish allergy rules it outFact: a seafood allergy doesn't raise your risk. Still tell us about every allergy.
2
Myth: every scan needs contrastFact: many don't. Most brain, spine and knee MRIs are done without it.
3
Myth: all contrast is the sameFact: they're different. A past CT reaction doesn't rule out MRI contrast.

Sources: AAAAI; RadiologyInfo.org (ACR, RSNA).
