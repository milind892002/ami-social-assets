November is Lung Cancer Awareness Month

A yearly low-dose CT can find lung cancer early, before symptoms.

In a large national trial, yearly low-dose CT screening lowered lung cancer deaths by about 20% in people at high risk, because it finds cancers while they are still small and treatable.

Sources: National Cancer Institute (NIH), National Lung Screening Trial; CDC.

Who is it for?

Yearly screening is advised for adults who:

Are 50 to 80 years old.

Have 20 pack-years of smoking.

Smoke now, or quit in the last 15 years.

Have no symptoms of lung cancer.

U.S. Preventive Services Task Force, 2021. The American Cancer Society (2023) also includes former smokers who quit more than 15 years ago. Guidelines differ; ask your doctor. A cough that won't go away or coughing up blood? See your doctor now.
